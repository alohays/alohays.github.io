Ch 2 — Action Modality 17 / 77

Two tokenization strategies

STRATEGY A

Bin discretization

Slice each continuous dim into 256 bins, treat each bin as a vocab token, predict with cross-entropy — same loop as an LLM.

  • Reuses LLM loss, optimizer, decoder verbatim
  • Quantization error; per-dim factorization

flagship — RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA

STRATEGY B

Continuous head

Keep the action continuous; bolt a separate diffusion / flow head on top of the VLM that denoises a whole action chunk at once.

  • Captures multimodal action distributions (no mode collapse)
  • Slower per call — but predicts a whole chunk at once

flagship — Diffusion Policy, ACT, π0

This is the #1 design choice behind every modern VLA. The next three slides take one example of each — and a 2025 hybrid that re-discretizes.
RT-1 [arXiv:2212.06817] · Diffusion Policy [arXiv:2303.04137] 17

Two strategies

Discretize the continuous vector and feed it to a language head, or keep it continuous and feed it to a denoiser/flow head. Strategy A buys you the entire LLM stack for free but pays in quantization error. Strategy B handles multimodal action distributions naturally (think left-vs-right reach), but you trade away the clean cross-entropy story. Almost every VLA paper is a vote for one of these two cells. The next three slides walk one example each — RT-1 (A), Diffusion Policy / ACT (B), and π0-FAST (a 2025 hybrid that quietly goes back to A).

  • Diffusion Policy — same DDPM you saw in Week 12, but the target is an action sequence, not pixels

Clip · Russ Tedrake @ Princeton Robotics Seminar

"Diffusion policy = sequence of future actions" · 20:02–21:05

DDPM → policy. Same noise schedule, same εΘ objective. Only the target tensor changes — from pixels to an action chunk.
ACT [arXiv:2304.13705] · Diffusion Policy [arXiv:2303.04137] 19

Action chunks

Predict a whole k-step sequence at once instead of one action per inference. Two reasons. (1) Real-time — you can run the heavy VLM once and replay the chunk open-loop. (2) Multimodality — with diffusion, the chunk captures "left reach" and "right reach" as two modes instead of averaging them into a useless middle path. The DDPM analogy is the load-bearing one for students who just learned diffusion: same epsilon-theta objective, same noise schedule — the only change is that we denoise an action chunk instead of an image.

  • Continuous → back to discrete, but each token carries more info

Clip · Sergey Levine @ TWiML #719

"A better way to compress actions" — FAST motivation · 39:17–40:25

The third option: continuous → back to discrete, but in a basis where each token carries real information.
arXiv:2501.09747 · Pertsch et al. "FAST: Efficient Action Tokenization for VLA Models" 20

FAST tokenizer

The 2025 twist on Strategy A. Take a chunk of continuous actions, run it through a Discrete Cosine Transform (energy concentrates in the low-frequency bins), quantize and drop the high-frequency tail (which is mostly noise anyway), and BPE-encode the result. The output is a discrete vocabulary again, but each token carries far more information than a single 256-bin quantization — sequences are roughly 5x shorter, so training and inference both speed up. Levine's TWiML quote is the cleanest statement of the intuition: "a better way to compress actions."
